NORAD F-16s Deploy to the High North for Operation Arctic Vanguard

By Michael ReynoldsSep 17, 2026 17:53 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

F-16 Fighting Falcons from Eielson Air Force Base, Alaska, have deployed to Goose Bay in Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada, to fly defense missions across the Arctic under Operation Arctic Vanguard.

The deployment, announced by the Alaskan NORAD Region, puts American fighters at a forward Canadian base covering the northern approaches to North America — the flight path Russian long-range bombers and cruise missiles would take in a conflict.

The Arctic has become a growing security concern. Russia has reopened Cold War-era bases along its northern coast, and China has declared itself a "near-Arctic state" while expanding its icebreaker fleet. NORAD, the joint U.S.-Canadian command, is responsible for detecting and intercepting threats in the region.

The F-16s at Eielson belong to the Air Force's Pacific air forces and are no strangers to Arctic operations, routinely training in extreme cold. Operating from Goose Bay extends their reach over the North Atlantic and eastern Arctic.

NORAD has not said how long the deployment will last or how many aircraft are involved.
