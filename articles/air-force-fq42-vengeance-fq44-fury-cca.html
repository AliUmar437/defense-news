Air Force Names Its New Drone Wingmen: FQ-42 Vengeance and FQ-44 Fury

James CarterSep 15, 2026 18:00 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

The U.S. Air Force has given names to its future drone wingmen. The service's first two Collaborative Combat Aircraft designs will be called the FQ-42 Vengeance and the FQ-44 Fury, Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink announced during a keynote at the Air and Space Forces Association's Air, Space and Cyber Conference on Sept. 14.

The naming is more than ceremony. It signals that the CCA program — the Air Force's effort to field thousands of AI-piloted drones that fly alongside crewed fighters — is moving from concept to real hardware with real designations.

CCAs are designed to be affordable, attritable unmanned aircraft that extend the reach of fighters like the F-35 and the future F-47. They can carry sensors and weapons deep into contested airspace, scout ahead, jam enemy radars, or strike targets — missions too dangerous or too numerous for manned jets alone.

The "FQ" designation follows Air Force convention: F for fighter, Q for unmanned. Two named designs suggest at least two vendors' aircraft are maturing in parallel, keeping competition alive as the program scales.

The Air Force has said it wants the first CCAs operational before the end of the decade, with the fleet eventually numbering in the thousands.
