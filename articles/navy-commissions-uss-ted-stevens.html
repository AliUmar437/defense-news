Navy Commissions Destroyer USS Ted Stevens in Alaska Ceremony

By Defense News StaffOct 05, 2026 17:09 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States
This article was drafted with AI assistance and validated by our editorial pipeline. See our Editorial Policy.

The Navy has commissioned its newest guided-missile destroyer, the USS Ted Stevens, into active service during a formal ceremony in Whittier, Alaska.

The ship honors the late Alaska senator Ted Stevens, a longtime advocate for defense spending and Arctic security whose name now sails on one of the fleet's most capable surface combatants, according to the Defense Department.

As an Arleigh Burke-class Flight III destroyer, the Ted Stevens carries the SPY-6 radar — a major leap in sensitivity over earlier ships — and the Aegis combat system that anchors U.S. naval air and missile defense. Flight III hulls are the Navy's answer to increasingly sophisticated missile threats.

Basing the commissioning in Alaska was itself a statement. The Arctic is becoming a zone of strategic competition, and the Navy has been working to prove it can operate its most advanced ships in the region's harsh conditions.

The Ted Stevens now joins the Pacific Fleet, where demand for missile-defense-capable destroyers continues to outstrip supply.
