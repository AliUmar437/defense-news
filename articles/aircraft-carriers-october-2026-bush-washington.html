Where the Carriers Are: Bush Takes Liberty in Phuket, Washington Covers Iran

David SullivanOct 06, 2026 22:11 UTC 3 min readRegion: Global

The USS George H.W. Bush pulled into Phuket, Thailand, on Oct. 4 for its first liberty call after 188 consecutive days at sea, leaving the USS George Washington as the Navy's only aircraft carrier supporting operations against Iran.

The visit, announced by U.S. 7th Fleet, marks the Bush's first port call since the carrier deployed from the continental United States on March 31. Guided-missile destroyer USS Delbert D. Black escorted the flattop from its previous station in the Arabian Sea. Reuters reported about 4,800 personnel were aboard for the rest-and-recreation break, with local media reporting the crew would be ashore from Oct. 4 to Oct. 9.

The Bush had been operating in U.S. Central Command's area of responsibility since April, when the United States briefly had three carriers on station in support of operations against Iran. "For the last six months, the strike group supported Operation Epic Fury and the U.S. naval blockade in the U.S. Central Command area of responsibility to secure maritime security and stability in the Middle East," according to the 7th Fleet statement. The carrier reached the theater via the Cape of Good Hope, avoiding the Red Sea and the Bab al-Mandeb Strait.

The departure leaves a single carrier strike group — led by the Washington — in the Middle East for the first time in months. The Wall Street Journal and USNI News have reported that the USS Theodore Roosevelt will deploy to the Middle East, which could briefly restore a multi-carrier presence, though any overlap would be temporary under normal rotation schedules.

Lincoln's marathon deployment

Across the Pacific, the USS Abraham Lincoln was holding off the coast of California to disembark Carrier Air Wing 9 and offload ammunition ahead of a homecoming this week, after the Washington relieved it in August. The Lincoln departed San Diego on Nov. 21, 2025; its return closes out a 321- to 322-day deployment — just short of the 326-day record the USS Gerald R. Ford set in May, and the second-longest deployment of the modern carrier era.

The marathon cruise drew scrutiny in August over grueling conditions aboard, including supply shortages and difficult living arrangements. Acting Secretary of the Navy Hung Cao called media accounts dishonest while acknowledging adjustments to meal plans and limited calls home, and wrote in a letter to Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand — obtained by CNN — that at least eight personnel across the strike group attempted suicide during the deployment.

A fleet stretched thin

The rest of the carrier fleet underscores the strain. The USS Ronald Reagan pulled into San Diego on Oct. 5 after departing Bremerton, Washington, on Sept. 30, following a 17-month drydocking availability during which she received an Unmanned Air Warfare Center to command and control the MQ-25 tanker drone — making her the second carrier with the upgrade, after the Theodore Roosevelt.

The USS Dwight D. Eisenhower returned to Naval Station Norfolk on Oct. 5 after an eight-day underway that ended with the loss of an EA-18G Growler on Sept. 28. The Navy attributed the mishap to a failure of the arresting gear; both crew members ejected safely and were rescued by helicopter, and the ship returned for a comprehensive assessment of its launch and recovery equipment.

And the USS Harry S. Truman moved into dry dock at Newport News Shipbuilding on Sept. 30 to begin her mid-life Refueling and Complex Overhaul. The Navy has eleven carriers on paper, but maintenance, refueling, and workups mean far fewer are deployable at any given time — a reality the 188-day Bush cruise and the 322-day Lincoln deployment make plain.
