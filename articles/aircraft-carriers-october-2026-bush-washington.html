Where the Carriers Are: Bush Takes Liberty in Phuket, Washington Covers Iran

David SullivanOct 06, 2026 22:11 UTC 1 min readRegion: Global

The USS George H.W. Bush has pulled into Phuket, Thailand, for its first liberty call after 188 days at sea — leaving the USS George Washington as the Navy's sole aircraft carrier supporting operations against Iran.

The snapshot, from The War Zone's regular carrier tracker, captures the strain on the fleet. With the Bush standing down after a grueling deployment, a single carrier is covering one of the world's most dangerous flashpoints.

Carrier deployments have been stretching longer as global demands pile up: deterring Iran, reassuring Pacific allies, and maintaining presence in the Mediterranean. The Navy has eleven carriers on paper, but maintenance backlogs mean far fewer are deployable at any given time.

The Bush's 188-day stretch at sea — more than six months — illustrates the tempo. Extended deployments wear on ships and sailors alike, and the Navy has acknowledged the pace is unsustainable.

The George Washington, forward-deployed to Japan, now carries the load in the Middle East alongside its escorts.
