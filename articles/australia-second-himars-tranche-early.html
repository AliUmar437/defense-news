Australia Receives Second HIMARS Tranche Two Years Ahead of Schedule

Michael ReynoldsOct 09, 2026 12:43 UTC 1 min readRegion: Indo-Pacific

The Australian Army has taken delivery of a second tranche of High Mobility Artillery Rocket System (HIMARS) launchers from the United States — two years ahead of schedule.

The early arrival, reported by Army Technology, is a notable bright spot in a defense procurement landscape more often defined by delays. For Australia, which is reorienting its military toward long-range strike in the Indo-Pacific, HIMARS is a centerpiece capability.

The truck-mounted system became famous in Ukraine, where its precision rockets devastated Russian ammunition depots and command posts far behind the front lines. Australia wants the same reach: the ability to strike targets at long distance from mobile, hard-to-find launchers.

Canberra's accelerated delivery suggests both strong U.S.-Australian industrial coordination and the priority Washington places on arming allies in the region. Further tranches are expected as Australia builds out its long-range fires enterprise, including domestic missile production efforts.

The Army has not said how many launchers were in this tranche.
