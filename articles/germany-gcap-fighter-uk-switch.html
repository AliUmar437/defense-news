Could Germany Join GCAP? UK Seeks to Advance Potential Fighter Switch

David SullivanOct 08, 2026 15:55 UTC 1 min readRegion: Europe

Britain is seeking to "progress" talks that could bring Germany into the Global Combat Air Programme (GCAP), the sixth-generation fighter project it leads with Japan and Italy.

The outreach, reported by Breaking Defense, follows the collapse of the Franco-German-Spanish FCAS program this summer — a breakup that left Berlin hunting for a next-generation fighter path and threw Europe's combat air plans into disarray.

GCAP aims to field a crewed sixth-generation fighter in the 2030s, built around stealth, long range, and manned-unmanned teaming with drone wingmen. Adding Germany — Europe's largest defense spender — would bring money, industrial weight, and political heft, but also new complications over workshare and requirements.

For London, German participation would validate GCAP as the European alternative after FCAS fell apart. For Berlin, it is one of several options on the table, alongside potential American offerings.

No formal negotiations have been announced, and officials on both sides are characterizing the contacts as exploratory.
