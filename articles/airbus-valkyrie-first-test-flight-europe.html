Airbus Flies Kratos Valkyrie Drone in Europe for the First Time, Targets 2029 Eurofighter Wingman

David SullivanOct 12, 2026 12:40 UTC 3 min readRegion: Europe

BERLIN - Airbus has carried out the first test flight in Europe of the U.S.-built Valkyrie combat drone it hopes to team with Eurofighter jets, a milestone in its bid to give Germany an uncrewed wingman aircraft by 2029.

The flight took place on Sept. 29 at a test site in northern Europe that the companies did not name, Airbus Defence and Space CEO Michael Schoellhorn told Reuters. The aircraft, which Airbus designates the U740 Valkyrie, launched from a rail using a rocket booster - no runway required - flew at various altitudes and speeds up to Mach 0.8, and had its mission retasked in flight.

"We tried all sorts of things and were very impressed, so it was a complete success," Schoellhorn said in an interview. He said the flight's primary significance was proving that the regulatory clearances needed to operate military drones in Europe's complex airspace can be secured.

The Valkyrie is based on the XQ-58 built by U.S.-based Kratos Defense, which has been flying in the United States since 2019. Under a partnership announced in 2025, Airbus plans to offer a combat-ready drone to the German Air Force by 2029, with the aircraft able to fly autonomously, under remote piloting or in teaming with Eurofighters. The European version is 9.1 meters long with an 8.2-meter wingspan, weighs about 3 metric tons at takeoff, has a range exceeding 5,000 kilometers, a ceiling of 45,000 feet, an internal weapons bay and pylons under its wings. The estimated unit cost is around 20 million euros.

Flying without the European brain - for now

Notably, this first flight did not yet carry Airbus's own mission system, called MARS. The software and hardware suite is the component that will let the aircraft fly missions autonomously and coordinate groups of drones, with a human kept "on the loop" to approve critical decisions. Airbus describes MARS as the sovereign European element of the program, giving Germany and other buyers full control of the software, data and mission hardware rather than dependence on the U.S. manufacturer.

Schoellhorn said the Valkyrie is expected to fly with MARS on board for its next test flight in the first quarter of 2027, and the first flight actually teamed with a Eurofighter is scheduled for later that year. No order for the drone has been placed yet - the 2029 combat-readiness target is an offer, not a contract.

A crowded race for loyal wingmen

Airbus is entering an increasingly crowded field. Boeing, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, General Atomics, Anduril Industries and Saab are all building uncrewed combat aircraft aimed at what could become a multibillion-dollar global market for collaborative combat aircraft - so-called loyal wingmen.

The regulatory angle matters especially in Germany. In 2013, Berlin cancelled the Northrop-built Euro Hawk drone after a wrangle over European civil certification rules, a failure that still haunts the country's drone ambitions. Schoellhorn framed the Sept. 29 flight as proof that such barriers can be overcome this time.

Why it matters: sovereignty is the product, the drone is the vehicle

Read past the flight and the story is really about a procurement model Europe keeps demanding: proven foreign hardware, European brains. Pairing Kratos's already-flying airframe with a European mission system Berlin fully controls is exactly what the Euro Hawk failure taught Germany to require - control of the software, data and weapons integration. The engineering risk is lower than a clean-sheet drone, but the hard part - MARS integration, Eurofighter teaming and European certification - all lies ahead. The early-2027 flight with MARS aboard will be the real milestone.

The 2029 target is aggressive, with no order placed and the teaming demonstration more than a year out. What may push Berlin is arithmetic: a wingman at roughly 20 million euros a unit offers combat mass at a fraction of a crewed fighter's cost, and Germany's air force, like every NATO air force, is short of affordable mass. If the MARS flight and the Eurofighter teaming demo hit their marks, the Valkyrie becomes a genuine procurement candidate - not just for Germany, but for every European air force adding drone mass without surrendering sovereignty over the software controlling it.
