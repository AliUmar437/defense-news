Ukraine's F-16 Fleet Faces Readiness Crisis as War Grinds On

By Defense News StaffSep 30, 2026 17:10 UTC 1 min readRegion: Europe
This article was drafted with AI assistance and validated by our editorial pipeline. See our Editorial Policy.

More than half of Ukraine's fleet of donated F-16 fighters is reportedly grounded, as the war grinds on and Western air-to-air missile stocks run low, according to The War Zone.

The readiness crisis threatens one of Ukraine's most important Western capabilities. The F-16s — donated by the Netherlands, Denmark, Norway, and Belgium — gave Kyiv its first modern fighters, used for air defense against Russian cruise missiles and drones.

But the jets are aging airframes, maintenance-intensive and dependent on a spare-parts pipeline that was never designed for wartime tempo. Keeping them flying requires constant Western support, and every grounded jet is one fewer interceptor against nightly Russian attacks.

Compounding the problem, stocks of Western air-to-air missiles are reportedly thinning — the same munitions the F-16s expend shooting down Shahed drones and cruise missiles.

Ukraine has asked partners for more jets, more parts, and more missiles. Whether the pipeline can keep pace is now an open question.
