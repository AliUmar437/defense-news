Navy-Marine Osprey Test Opens New Anti-Submarine Chapter

Robert HayesSep 23, 2026 16:29 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

A joint Navy-Marine Corps team has executed the first lot acceptance test of sonobuoys dropped from an MV-22B Osprey tiltrotor off San Clemente Island, California — laying the groundwork for a new era of tiltrotor anti-submarine warfare.

The test, announced by the Defense Department, proved the Osprey can dispense the acoustic sensors that hunt submarines. Sonobuoys listen for undersea contacts and relay what they hear to aircraft overhead; until now, dropping them was the job of fixed-wing patrol planes and helicopters.

An ASW-capable Osprey would be a significant addition. The tiltrotor flies twice as fast and far as a helicopter, letting it sprint to a suspected submarine contact and then hover to prosecute it — combining the best of both worlds.

With Chinese and Russian submarine activity rising, the Navy is hungry for more ASW capacity across distributed forces.

The test clears the way for expanded tactical use of sonobuoys across the Osprey fleet.
