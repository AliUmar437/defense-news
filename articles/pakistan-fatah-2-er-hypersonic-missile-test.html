Pakistan Test-Fires Extended-Range Fatah-2 Hypersonic Ballistic Missile

James CarterOct 15, 2026 12:40 UTC 3 min readRegion: Indo-Pacific

Pakistan's military said Tuesday it had successfully test-fired an extended-range version of the Fatah-2 ballistic missile, describing it as a hypersonic weapon capable of defeating enemy missile defenses and striking targets deep inside adversary territory.

The Inter-Services Public Relations (ISPR), the military's media wing, said the Fatah-2 Extended Range (ER) weapon system is equipped with advanced navigation and state-of-the-art avionics. "It is capable of engaging targets with high precision and defeating the enemy's missile defence systems due to its unique trajectory and manoeuvrability features," the ISPR said in a statement.

The military did not disclose the missile's range or release flight data. ISPR said only that the Fatah-2 ER's extended range "will further enhance the army's reach and capability of engaging deep targets."

The launch was witnessed by the commander of the Army Rocket Force Command, Lieutenant General Nauman Zakaria, along with senior Pakistan Army officers, scientists and engineers. The ARFC commander praised the dedication and technical prowess of the scientists and engineers behind the program. President Asif Ali Zardari, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, and Chief of Army Staff and Chief of Defence Forces Field Marshal Asim Munir, along with the service chiefs, congratulated the teams on the successful test.

Second test in a week

The Fatah-2 ER launch followed a test last week of an extended version of the Fatah-4 ground-launched cruise missile, which the ISPR described as indigenously developed and equipped with advanced avionics and navigational aids capable of engaging targets at extended ranges with high precision and lethality. That test also validated additional technical parameters of sub-systems incorporated for improved accuracy and survivability, according to ISPR.

The back-to-back launches continue a steady drumbeat of Pakistani missile activity. In September 2025, the army conducted a training launch of the newly developed Fatah-4 at a range of 750 kilometers, describing it as a terrain-hugging missile designed to evade enemy missile defenses. In May, the army conducted a successful test firing of the Fatah-IV ground-launched cruise missile.

A note on the "hypersonic" label: ISPR describes the Fatah-2 ER as a hypersonic ballistic missile, and says its trajectory and maneuverability are designed to defeat interceptors. Ballistic missiles reenter the atmosphere at hypersonic speeds as a matter of physics, so the meaningful part of the claim is the maneuverability — the aerodynamic pull-up maneuvers and course corrections that would make interception harder. Without released flight data, that part of the claim cannot be independently verified.

Why it matters

Two extended-range conventional strike systems tested within about a week — a cruise missile and a ballistic missile — points to an accelerated campaign to extend the Pakistan Army's conventional deep-strike reach. The Fatah family, developed under the army's artillery and now rocket-force command, gives Pakistan a land-based precision-strike option that does not depend on its air force's stand-off weapons.

The emphasis on "defeating enemy missile defence" is the tell. The regional missile competition with India increasingly turns on survivability — maneuverability, trajectory shaping, and low-observable flight profiles — rather than raw range alone, as both sides field layered air-defense systems. Pakistan's ISPR messaging is aimed squarely at that argument: that its new missiles will still get through.

What to watch next: whether ISPR releases a range figure or launch video for the Fatah-2 ER — it withheld both this time, notable for a test the country's top leadership publicly celebrated. The next signal would be an induction announcement or a formal unveiling of the Army Rocket Force Command's operational structure, which would turn two years of testing into a declared capability.
