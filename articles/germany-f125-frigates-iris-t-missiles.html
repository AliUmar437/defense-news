Germany Finally Gives Its F125 Frigates Real Teeth with IRIS-T Missiles

Robert HayesOct 02, 2026 21:01 UTC 3 min readRegion: Europe

Germany is finally giving its biggest warships real air defenses. On Oct. 2, 2026, Diehl Defence and the federal procurement office BAAINBw signed an adaptation development contract to fit the naval version of the IRIS-T SLM surface-to-air missile to the German Navy's four F125 frigates — nine days after the Bundestag's budget committee approved the program on Sept. 23.

Each 7,200-ton frigate will receive two deck-mounted launchers holding 16 missiles apiece: 32 interceptors per ship, in vertical-launch canisters, against aircraft, helicopters, cruise missiles, and drones. The missile reaches 40 kilometers out and 20 kilometers up, guided by datalink with terminal self-homing.

The four ships of the Baden-Württemberg class — Baden-Württemberg, Nordrhein-Westfalen, Sachsen-Anhalt, Rheinland-Pfalz — were built for stabilization and crisis-management missions, and their armament showed it: short-range anti-ship missiles and two RIM-116 RAM point-defense launchers on a hull the size of a destroyer. The gap became a public embarrassment in 2024, when Baden-Württemberg detoured around Africa rather than transit the Red Sea on its return from an Indo-Pacific deployment.

From concept to firing in ten months

The program is moving fast because the hard part is already done. In October 2025, during a naval firing exercise off Norway, engineers bolted a demonstrator launcher to the C-deck of Baden-Württemberg and engaged multiple targets using the ship's own radar data — going from concept to live firing in under ten months.

The naval adaptation strips out the ground system's support legs and hydraulics to save weight and deck space. Functional prototypes are expected in 2027, with sea trials and the first operational test launches the same year.

Diehl's own description of the goal is deliberately modest: "The aim is to make the system mission-capable on the German Navy's F125 frigates." But the engineering is not trivial. The naval IRIS-T SLM drops the ground launcher's support legs and hydraulics, fires from a compact vertical canister, and stays in contact with the ship via datalink until its own seeker takes over for the terminal phase — a fire-and-update architecture built for saturation attacks by drones and sea-skimmers.

A missile with a combat record

The IRIS-T SLM is not a paper missile. German-supplied batteries in Ukraine have intercepted Shahed drones and Kh-101 and Kalibr cruise missiles at ranges up to 40 kilometers, and Berlin has explicitly cited that operational record in justifying the frigate upgrade.

The move fits Germany's broader post-2022 naval rearmament — new F126 frigates and Type 212CD submarines with Norway are in the pipeline alongside missile upgrades across the fleet. But the F125 fix is the most symbolically loaded: the ships were designed in an era when Berlin assumed its navy would police crises, not fight wars.

For a navy that watched its F124 frigate Hessen swat Houthi drones over the Red Sea under the EU's Operation Aspides while its newest, largest frigates lacked comparable defenses, the contract closes what The War Zone called one of the most conspicuous weaknesses in a Western fleet. Thirty-two missiles per ship will not make the F125 an air-defense destroyer. But it will, at last, give it teeth.
