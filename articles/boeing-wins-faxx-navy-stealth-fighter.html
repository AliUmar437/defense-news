Boeing Wins F/A-XX Contract for Navy's Next-Generation Stealth Fighter

By Defense News StaffSep 29, 2026 21:40 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

Boeing has won the competition to build the F/A-XX, the Navy's next-generation stealth fighter that will eventually replace the F/A-18 Super Hornet aboard U.S. aircraft carriers.

The award, reported by The War Zone, ends months of uncertainty around a program the Pentagon had at one point tried to freeze. F/A-XX has been among the most closely watched contract decisions in military aviation, with the future of naval air power hanging on its outcome.

The new fighter is expected to fly farther, carry more weapons, and operate alongside uncrewed "loyal wingman" drones as part of the Navy's Next Generation Air Dominance family of systems. It will form the manned centerpiece of the carrier air wing of the 2030s, working with the MQ-25 Stingray tanker drone and a growing fleet of collaborative combat aircraft.

For Boeing's defense business, the win is a major reversal of fortune. The company's fighter portfolio had been thinning as F/A-18 production winds down, and losing F/A-XX would have effectively ended Boeing as a builder of manned tactical fighters.

The Navy has not yet released the contract value or a first-flight timeline. Expect those details — and the losing bidders' next moves — to emerge in the coming weeks.
