Boeing Wins F/A-XX Contract for Navy's Next-Generation Stealth Fighter

Michael ReynoldsSep 29, 2026 21:40 UTC 3 min readRegion: United States

Boeing has won the Navy's F/A-XX contract, the award for the service's sixth-generation carrier fighter, in a decision announced Sept. 29 that hands the company both of the Pentagon's next-generation fighter programs.

The contract covers the full-scale development phase and is valued at more than $20 billion, including multiple test aircraft for ground, airworthiness, systems, and weapons integration testing. Boeing beat Lockheed Martin and Northrop Grumman. Northrop has requested a formal debriefing with Navy officials — the customary step before any protest.

Why Boeing won

According to people familiar with the competition, Boeing prevailed on the strength of years of investment, decades of experience building carrier-based jets, and a design approach that matched the Pentagon's demand for fighters that are easier to upgrade across long service lives. The Pentagon required all three competitors to use common design standards so later modifications could be made quickly as technology evolves.

Boeing also leaned hard on digital design tools, letting engineers, military officials, and maintainers test changes virtually before committing to hardware — a pitch that resonated after the industry's recent struggles with cost overruns on fixed-price development programs.

"Now all of a sudden, it controls the next half century and beyond of US tactical aircraft," Richard Aboulafia of AeroDynamic Advisory said of Boeing. "There's nothing like that in history."

Two sixth-generation programs, one builder

The F/A-XX award follows Boeing's March 2025 win of the Air Force's F-47, the manned fighter at the heart of Next Generation Air Dominance. With both programs, Boeing now holds the entire American sixth-generation fighter roadmap — a position Lockheed Martin held in the fifth generation with the F-22 and F-35.

"It's an honour to be selected to produce the Navy's first sixth-generation platform," Steve Parker, chief executive of Boeing Defense, Space & Security, said. "Delivering two advanced fighters in parallel was always our plan, and we invested accordingly."

The fighter will be built in St. Louis, Missouri, where construction continues on a secure manufacturing facility tied to an all-digital design and production system.

What F/A-XX is for

The Navy describes F/A-XX as a sixth-generation strike fighter built around open mission systems architecture, advanced sensors, and greater operational reach — designed to fly farther, carry more, and operate alongside uncrewed collaborative combat aircraft. It is intended to begin augmenting, and ultimately replace, the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet and EA-18G Growler, whose airframes are approaching structural and survivability limits against modern air defenses.

On the same day as the award, the Navy issued Boeing a contract to begin planning the shutdown of Super Hornet and Growler production — a signal of how the service intends to hand the St. Louis workforce and tooling from one generation to the next without a capability gap.

The road ahead

Technical and programmatic details remain classified under national security rules. The program had at times looked vulnerable, with officials openly questioning whether the industrial base could handle two advanced fighters — plus Northrop Grumman's B-21 bomber — simultaneously. Awarding both fighters to one company may ease that strain through shared knowledge and economies of scale, though it concentrates enormous risk in a single contractor whose defense division has absorbed billions in losses on fixed-price programs.

First-flight timelines and production quantities have not been disclosed. The losing bidders' next moves — and the contract's finer print — should surface in the coming weeks.
