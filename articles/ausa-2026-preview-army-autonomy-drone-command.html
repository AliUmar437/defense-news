New Leaders, New Drone Command: What to Expect from AUSA 2026

James CarterOct 09, 2026 16:07 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

The Association of the U.S. Army's annual conference opens with the service at an inflection point: new leaders at the top, a new drone command standing up, and big questions about which modernization programs survive.

The Army's autonomy push will loom largest, Breaking Defense reports. After watching drones reshape the war in Ukraine, the service is reorganizing how it buys, fields, and fights with uncrewed systems — and standing up dedicated formations to do it.

Also under the microscope: the future of the Army's marquee modernization programs. With budgets under pressure and priorities shifting toward drones, long-range fires, and air defense, some legacy efforts face hard scrutiny from the new leadership.

Industry will be listening closely. AUSA is where the Army signals, often bluntly, what it wants to buy — and just as importantly, what it no longer wants. Expect contract announcements, prototype unveilings, and carefully worded warnings to programs falling behind.

Defense News will be covering the conference's major developments as they happen.
