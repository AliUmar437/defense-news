Taiwan's First F-16 Block 70 Fighters Finally Arrive

David SullivanOct 02, 2026 16:34 UTC 3 min readRegion: Indo-Pacific

Taiwan's first two F-16 Block 70 fighters touched down at Chihhang Air Base on Oct. 2, ending a wait of nearly seven years and opening the long-delayed renewal of the island's fighter fleet.

The single-seat F-16Cs, serials 6727 and 6728, landed near Taitung on the island's southeast coast at about midday, escorted for the final stretch by Taiwanese F-16s and T-BE-5 Brave Eagle trainers flying in formation. They are the first of 66 Block 70 aircraft Taiwan ordered from the United States in 2019 under an approximately $8 billion deal known locally as the Peace Phoenix Soaring project.

A long road from Greenville

The jets left Lockheed Martin's F-16 plant in Greenville, South Carolina, on Aug. 17, routing through Fort Worth, Hawaii, and Guam with support from a U.S. Air Force KC-135 tanker. They then sat in Hawaii for more than six weeks — held up first by weather, then by unspecified technical issues, and finally by a U.S.-imposed pause over the optics of a late-September meeting between President Donald Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping.

In a break with past practice, the aircraft had their U.S. Air Force markings removed before leaving Hawaii, revealing the Republic of China Air Force roundel underneath. On previous F-16 deliveries in the 1990s and 2000s, Taiwan's markings were kept covered until the jets arrived home, a nod to political sensitivities that this time was set aside.

The first Taiwanese Block 70 had been unveiled in Greenville in March 2025. Getting from rollout to delivery took another 18 months of testing and calibration to integrate Taiwan-specific systems, wiring, and software — on top of earlier supply-chain disruptions that had already pushed the program years behind its original schedule.

Why it matters

Taiwan's fighter fleet is among the oldest in the region, and its pilots fly near-daily sorties shadowing Chinese military aircraft operating around the island. The tempo has worn airframes down, and the Block 70s bring relief: Northrop Grumman's AN/APG-83 active electronically scanned array radar, L3Harris's Viper Shield electronic warfare suite, conformal fuel tanks, and General Electric F110 engines put Taiwanese Vipers close to fifth-generation standards in sensing and networking, with compatibility for weapons including the AIM-120D, AGM-88, and Harpoon.

When all 66 arrive, Taiwan will operate more than 200 F-16Vs, counting the 139 older F-16A/Bs upgraded to a similar standard under a program completed in 2023. The new jets will equip the 7th Tactical Fighter Wing, stood up in December 2023, as Chihhang shifts from a training base to a frontline combat station.

Beijing objects, Taipei presses on

China's Foreign Ministry restated its opposition to U.S. arms sales to Taiwan, with spokesman Guo Jiakun calling the position "consistent and clear." President Lai Ching-te, posting on social media, called the delivery an important milestone for air defense and said investments that raise the cost of any invasion help deter miscalculation.

Defense Minister Wellington Koo told lawmakers that additional jets would arrive before the end of the year, with more than 60 aircraft on the U.S. production line — some already at the delivery stage. After years in which the program became a test of whether approved arms sales could turn into real capability, the two Vipers on the ramp at Chihhang are the first tangible answer.
