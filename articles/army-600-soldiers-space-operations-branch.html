US Army Creates First Permanent Enlisted Cadre for Space Operations Branch

David SullivanOct 14, 2026 12:41 UTC 3 min readRegion: North America

The U.S. Army has officially transferred more than 600 soldiers into its new Space Operations Branch, creating the branch's first permanent enlisted cadre and marking one of the most significant reorganizations of the Army's space mission in decades.

The soldiers voluntarily applied and were selected to reclassify as 40D Tactical Space Operations Specialists, the service announced. Nearly 3,000 soldiers applied for the first tranche, according to the Army, drawn from across the force — including Military Intelligence, Air Defense Artillery and the Signal Corps — though the 40D specialty is open to applicants from any branch.

The reclassification took effect October 1. The 40D enlisted soldiers join the existing 40A Space Operations officers in the Space Operations Branch, which the Army established as an independent branch in June, placing it alongside long-standing branches such as Infantry, Aviation and the Chemical Corps.

An end to the rotational model

Until now, the Army filled its space assignments by temporarily pulling soldiers from other specialties — a system commanders said worked in the past but steadily drained institutional knowledge as trained soldiers returned to their original branches.

Lt. Gen. John L. Rafferty, commanding general of the U.S. Army Space and Missile Defense Command, said the rotational approach "will not meet the demands of the future," arguing the service needs a professional structure with real career progression for soldiers whose work increasingly shapes how ground units fight. The 40D designation gives space-trained enlisted soldiers a permanent home, with defined training and promotion paths.

Training for the career field takes place at the Space and Missile Defense Center of Excellence in Colorado Springs, Colorado, where the new soldiers will learn satellite operations, missile warning, positioning and timing support, and the space-integration missions that underpin modern land warfare.

Applications opened in January

The Army opened applications for the specialty in January to soldiers in grades E-4 through E-9 across the active-duty force, National Guard and Army Reserve. The strong response — nearly 3,000 applicants for roughly 600 slots — suggests the branch tapped into a real appetite among enlisted soldiers for a career path in a domain that has grown more central with each major conflict.

While the 40A and 40D designations are new, the Army's institutional roots in space run deep. In October 1984, the five-person Army Staff Field Element was stood up to formulate space policy and monitor space-related education — the seed of what has become a full branch with its own insignia, unveiled earlier this month, combining a globe, sword, arrow and an orbital lightning bolt.

Why it matters

Every modern ground war is now a space war in disguise. Satellite navigation times artillery strikes, missile warning protects forward bases, and assured communications keep dispersed formations alive — all of it vulnerable to jamming and anti-satellite attack. For years the Army treated space as a capability someone else provided, borrowing personnel to sit at consoles designed elsewhere.

Creating a permanent enlisted cadre is the service admitting that era is over. The signal to adversaries is straightforward: the Army intends to fight contested in orbit, not just borrow from it. The signal to the force is about talent retention — skilled space operators finally have a career, not a temporary assignment. In the next crisis over the straits of Hormuz or the Taiwan Strait, the soldiers tasked with keeping Army systems alive in a degraded space environment will now belong to space, not pass through it.
