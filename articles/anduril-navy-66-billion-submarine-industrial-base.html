Anduril and U.S. Navy Announce $6.6 Billion Push to Expand Submarine Industrial Base

James CarterOct 06, 2026 21:24 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

Anduril Industries and the U.S. Navy are putting a combined $6.6 billion into expanding America's submarine industrial base, in one of the largest private-public shipbuilding investments in decades.

The centerpiece, announced October 6, is Arsenal-2, a new software-defined shipyard backed by a $3.7 billion private capital investment from Anduril. The facility will manufacture critical components for the Virginia-class attack submarine, adding badly needed capacity to a strained supply chain, according to Naval News.

U.S. submarine production has been the pacing problem for naval planners for years. The Navy wants to build two Virginia-class boats a year while simultaneously starting the Columbia-class ballistic missile submarine — a workload the current industrial base has struggled to meet, with suppliers stretched thin and skilled labor in short supply.

Anduril, best known for drones and autonomous systems, is now moving directly into heavy manufacturing. A software-defined shipyard implies heavy automation, digital design tools, and production methods borrowed from the tech industry rather than traditional shipbuilding.

If it works, the model could reshape how America builds warships. If it stumbles, the submarine programs it is meant to rescue will feel it first.
