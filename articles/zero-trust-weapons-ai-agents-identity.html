Zero Trust Gets Harder as Every Weapon and AI Agent Needs an Identity

David SullivanOct 09, 2026 13:20 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

The Pentagon's Zero Trust cybersecurity drive is entering its hardest phase yet — extending identity-based security beyond people and laptops to every weapon system, sensor, and AI agent on the network.

That was the takeaway from a Breaking Defense webinar, where experts described the next stage of the effort: in a future force where drones, autonomous vehicles, and AI software all act on data, each one needs a verifiable identity before it can be trusted.

Zero Trust assumes no user or device is trustworthy by default, verifying every access request. Applying that to weapons is a different order of difficulty. A fighter jet, a missile battery, or an AI targeting assistant cannot simply be issued a password — their identities must be baked into hardware and software, resilient to jamming, spoofing, and capture.

The stakes are rising as the military networks more of its arsenal together. A single compromised node in a connected force could poison targeting data or blind commanders — which is exactly what adversaries are trying to achieve.

Officials say the identity problem is now one of the pacing challenges for the Pentagon's broader digital modernization.
