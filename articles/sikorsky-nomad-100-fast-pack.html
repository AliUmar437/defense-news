Sikorsky Packs Four Nomad 100 Drones Into One Shipping Container for Rapid Combat Deployment

Robert HayesOct 13, 2026 12:39 UTC 3 min readRegion: North America

WASHINGTON - Sikorsky has packed a small drone air force into a shipping container.

The Lockheed Martin subsidiary introduced the Nomad 100 Fast Pack on Monday, a containerized vertical-takeoff-and-landing uncrewed aircraft kit unveiled as the Association of the U.S. Army's annual conference opened in Washington. A single standard 20-foot ISO container holds four Nomad 100 drones along with the mission-command software, power, communications and sustainment equipment needed to fly them - a complete expeditionary drone detachment that fits on the back of a truck.

Ready in minutes, no runway needed

The headline figure is speed. Sikorsky says that in its planned configuration, two of the four aircraft can be mission ready in less than 15 minutes, with the other two available within four to six hours. The system launches without a runway, from sites such as naval vessels, tactical vehicle staging areas and improvised landing zones. After a mission, crews can repack the container, load it onto a compatible vehicle and move to a new site.

Depending on how a customer configures it, the kit can run intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance and targeting missions, along with light attack and contested logistics - the expeditionary ISR-T, strike and multirole portfolio the Pentagon has been urging industry to scale up.

"Military leaders are challenging industry to expand the inventory of uncrewed aircraft, and Sikorsky is delivering proven autonomous air capability with speed and scale, offering field-ready solutions to deploy and sustain those systems where they are needed most," Rich Benton, Sikorsky's vice president and general manager, said in the company's announcement.

A proven airframe in new packaging

The aircraft inside the container is not a clean-sheet design. The Nomad 100, with an 18-foot wingspan, is a Group 3 uncrewed aircraft under Pentagon classification, built around Sikorsky's rotor-blown-wing design - helicopter-style vertical lift and hover paired with the speed, range and endurance of a fixed-wing aircraft - and flown with the company's MATRIX autonomy software. It completed initial ground and flight testing in July as part of DARPA's Evade demonstration, Aviation Week reported, and is a larger sibling of the Nomad 50, which first flew in March 2025.

Sikorsky has not disclosed the Nomad 100's payload, range or endurance, Military Times noted - a deliberate gap that keeps the focus on what the company is really selling here. It is not pitching the airframe; it is pitching the logistics wrapper.

Why it matters

The Fast Pack is a bet that the Pentagon's drone problem is no longer about building better aircraft - it is about getting good-enough aircraft forward fast. Two years of watching uncrewed systems in Ukraine, the Red Sea and now the war with Iran have taught the U.S. military that runway-dependent drone bases are fragile and that repositioning speed matters as much as payload. A containerized kit that arrives with its own command, power and sustainment gear is designed for exactly that lesson.

The naval angle is worth watching. The system is explicitly designed to launch from ships, which makes the Fast Pack a cross-service product rather than an Army-only one - a notable hedge at a time when the services are competing for drone funding. Group 3 is also a deliberate choice: large enough to carry real ISR and light-attack payloads, small enough to deploy by the handful.

What to watch: whether the Army - or the Navy - converts the AUSA pitch into actual procurement. Sikorsky has not named a customer, a price or a delivery timeline, and containerized drone kits from other vendors have struggled to move from conference-floor concept to program of record. The next sign of traction will be a military unit or service acquisition office signing for the first kit.
