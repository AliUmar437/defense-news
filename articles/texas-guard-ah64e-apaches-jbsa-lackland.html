Texas Guard's AH-64E Apaches Relocate to JBSA-Lackland in Army Reshuffle

By David SullivanOct 06, 2026 15:00 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

The Texas Army National Guard's AH-64E Apache battalion has begun relocating to Joint Base San Antonio-Lackland, part of a broader Army plan to reformat its aviation forces.

The 1st Battalion, 149th Aviation Regiment — an attack reconnaissance battalion under the 36th Combat Aviation Brigade — started the move recently, according to the Defense Department.

The AH-64E Apache Guardian is the latest version of the Army's legendary attack helicopter, with improved sensors, networking, and the ability to control drones from the cockpit. Guard Apaches give states a potent capability for both federal missions and domestic emergencies.

The relocation is part of the Army's ongoing effort to consolidate aviation units at major installations, where maintenance, training ranges, and support infrastructure are concentrated.

The move is expected to take months to complete.
