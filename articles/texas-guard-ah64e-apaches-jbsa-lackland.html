Texas Guard's AH-64E Apaches Relocate to JBSA-Lackland in Army Reshuffle

David SullivanOct 06, 2026 15:00 UTC 3 min readRegion: United States

The first three AH-64E Apache attack helicopters assigned to the Texas Army National Guard's 1st Battalion, 149th Aviation Regiment touched down at Kelly Field, Joint Base San Antonio-Lackland, on Sept. 24, 2026, opening a months-long relocation that folds the Guard battalion into one of the Army's most contested force-structure overhauls in decades.

The move places the 1-149th — a National Guard reconnaissance battalion under the 36th Combat Aviation Brigade, 36th Infantry Division — at Kelly Field as part of what the Army calls its reformatting plan, the service's broad effort to reshape aviation around fewer, more modern aircraft.

For the battalion, the relocation is the second phase of a transformation that began in February. On Feb. 3, 2026, the 1st Battalion, 101st Aviation Regiment — the active-duty "No Mercy" battalion at Fort Campbell, Kentucky — transferred its first two AH-64E Apache Version 4 helicopters to the Texas and Mississippi Guards, with the 1-149th receiving the airframes as the 101st made room for the incoming Version 6 upgrade.

Lt. Col. Cody Anfinson, the 1-149th's commander, described the handover as a milestone for a unit with long Apache roots. The battalion retired the last AH-64A in the entire Army back in July 2012 — tail number 90-00451, flown out of Ellington Field in Houston — and has waited more than a decade to step into the Army's latest attack helicopter.

A Guard battalion at the center of Army reform

The relocation lands the 1-149th squarely in the middle of the Army Transformation Initiative, launched by a Pentagon memo in April 2025 and now being executed across the aviation branch. Under the plan, the active-duty AH-64E fleet is shrinking from 408 airframes to 240, with the newest helicopters transferring into the National Guard. Eleven active air cavalry squadrons and two reserve expeditionary combat aviation brigades are being disbanded, older AH-64D models are being decommissioned outright, and roughly 6,500 aviation positions are being cut across 2026 and 2027, according to figures reported by Aviation Week and cited by Defense Express.

Army leaders have framed the cuts as a trade: fewer helicopters, but newer ones, concentrated where they can be sustained. The Guard, with its deep maintenance experience, is being positioned as the long-term home for the Apache even as the active force pivots toward unmanned systems and next-generation aircraft.

The 1-149th's structure reflects the Guard model. Its headquarters sits at Ellington Field Joint Reserve Base in Houston; Company A belongs to the Mississippi Army National Guard, while Companies B and C are Texas units. Consolidating the battalion at Kelly Field brings its aircraft, maintainers, and crews under one roof at an installation with deep aviation roots — Kelly Field has launched military aircraft for more than a century.

Version 4 today, Version 6 tomorrow

The helicopters arriving in San Antonio are AH-64E Version 4 aircraft, the configuration the 101st is shedding as it converts to Version 6 — a standard that brings improved rotor blades, larger fuel capacity, and a large-area cockpit display, according to Boeing. For a Guard battalion that flew the oldest Apaches in the Army just over a decade ago, the Version 4 aircraft represent a generational jump in sensors, networking, and lethality.

The relocation is expected to take months to complete as aircraft, equipment, and personnel flow south from the battalion's current stations. When finished, the 1-149th will be one of the Guard's most modern attack battalions — and a test case for the Army's bet that the National Guard can carry a larger share of the service's attack aviation future.
