USS George Washington Recovers Its 200,000th Aircraft in 34 Years at Sea

David SullivanAug 20, 2026 17:25 UTC 1 min readRegion: Indo-Pacific

The aircraft carrier USS George Washington has recovered its 200,000th fixed-wing aircraft, a milestone reached while underway in the Indian Ocean after 34 years of continuous service.

Every "trap" — the controlled crash of a jet catching an arresting wire at 150 miles per hour — is a small feat of precision. Two hundred thousand of them represents decades of round-the-clock flight operations, from combat missions to humanitarian relief.

The George Washington, forward-deployed to Japan, is the Navy's only permanently forward-based carrier, putting it at the center of American power projection in the Indo-Pacific. It is currently supporting operations against Iran, the only carrier doing so after the USS George H.W. Bush stood down.

Nimitz-class carriers like the Washington are being succeeded by the Ford class, but with a service life measured in decades, the ship will remain a fleet mainstay for years.

The Navy did not identify the milestone aircraft or its squadron.
