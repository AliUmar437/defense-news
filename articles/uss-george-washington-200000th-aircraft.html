USS George Washington Recovers Its 200,000th Aircraft in 34 Years at Sea

David SullivanAug 20, 2026 17:25 UTC 3 min readRegion: Indo-Pacific

An F-35C Lightning II caught the number three wire aboard USS George Washington on Aug. 18, 2026, and made history doing it: the 200,000th fixed-wing aircraft recovery in the carrier's 34 years at sea.

The milestone trap happened as the ship steamed in the Indian Ocean. The pilot was Lt. Steven Frazier of Strike Fighter Squadron 147, the "Argonauts," flying a routine sortie in the U.S. 7th Fleet area of operations.

Rear Adm. Jeffrey L. Heames, commander of Carrier Strike Group 5, called the number a testament to the enduring strength of American naval aviation and the professionalism of the sailors who make it possible, adding that U.S. carriers remain ready to launch and recover aircraft anywhere, anytime.

Thirty-four years of traps

George Washington was commissioned on July 4, 1992 — the sixth Nimitz-class carrier — and has been the Navy's only permanently forward-deployed carrier, homeported in Japan, since 2008. The aircraft it has recovered trace the evolution of naval aviation itself: F-14 Tomcats in the 1990s, legacy F/A-18 Hornets, then Super Hornets, EA-18G Growlers, E-2 Hawkeyes, and now the F-35C.

A trap is a violent, precise event. The aircraft snags a steel arresting cable with its tailhook at more than 140 miles per hour; a carrier at full tempo can land one aircraft per minute, roughly 70 a day. Two hundred thousand of them represents decades of flight-deck crews working in one of the most dangerous industrial environments on earth.

"For the George Washington, more than anything, it shows consistency over the last 34 years to be able to complete whatever mission the country calls upon it to do," Frazier said. "For me personally, it shows that if this aircraft carrier launches me off of the ship, I'll be able to come back and land on it."

The milestone also quietly underlines the durability of the Nimitz design. While the newer Ford class has wrestled with its launch and recovery systems, George Washington's counter kept turning — 200,000 recoveries without fanfare, on a hull commissioned when the Soviet Union had been gone barely seven months.

George Washington is the flagship of Carrier Strike Group 5, the Navy's only permanently forward-deployed strike group, homeported at Yokosuka, Japan. The 200,000th trap is as much a tribute to the ship's company — the catapult and arresting-gear crews, the ordnancemen, the controllers in the tower — as to the pilots. A carrier's trap count is the one statistic that cannot be spun: every number on that board was a real airplane, a real deck, a real wire.

Straight into the fight

The celebration was brief. The ship's 7th Fleet patrol had already kept it at sea for months; the Arabian Sea tasking extended that deployment into one of the longest continuous forward operations of the carrier's career.

Two days after the milestone, George Washington sailed into the Arabian Sea to take over the Middle East mission from USS Abraham Lincoln, whose marathon deployment — including combat operations against Iran — was ending. The move left a temporary carrier gap in the Western Pacific, a trade the Navy accepted to keep pressure on in the Central Command theater.
