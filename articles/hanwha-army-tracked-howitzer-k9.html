Hanwha Eyes Army's Tracked Howitzer Program After Wheeled-Arty Win

James CarterOct 09, 2026 14:09 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

South Korea's Hanwha is setting its sights on the U.S. Army's tracked howitzer program, planning to offer its K9A1 or K9A2 self-propelled guns for the competition.

A Hanwha executive told reporters of the plan, according to Breaking Defense — a move that follows the company's victory in the Army's wheeled howitzer competition. Winning both would make Hanwha the dominant foreign supplier of American artillery.

The K9 Thunder is one of the world's most successful artillery systems, in service with South Korea and exported to Poland, Norway, Finland, Australia, and others. The K9A2 variant adds automation, including an automatic loader that cuts crew size and raises the rate of fire.

The Army's artillery modernization has gained urgency from Ukraine, where massed tube artillery remains decisive. The service wants longer range, faster shooting, and guns that can move before counter-battery fire arrives.

Competition details and timelines have not been released.
