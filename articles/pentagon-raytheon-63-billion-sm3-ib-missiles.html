Pentagon, Raytheon Sign Up to $6.3 Billion Multi-Year Deal for SM-3 IB Missiles

By Defense News StaffOct 08, 2026 22:07 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States
This article was drafted with AI assistance and validated by our editorial pipeline. See our Editorial Policy.

The Pentagon has signed a multi-year contract with Raytheon worth up to $6.3 billion for SM-3 Block IB interceptors, locking in long-term production of one of the Navy's most important missile-defense weapons.

The deal, reported by Breaking Defense, lands just months after the Defense Department and Raytheon inked a framework agreement aimed at boosting output of the air-defense munition. Multi-year contracts of this kind let the Pentagon order at steadier, higher rates while giving industry the confidence to invest in its own production lines.

The SM-3 Block IB is the backbone of sea-based ballistic missile defense. Fired from Aegis-equipped destroyers and cruisers — and from the Aegis Ashore sites in Europe — it intercepts short- to intermediate-range ballistic missiles outside the atmosphere, using a kinetic warhead that destroys its target by collision rather than explosives.

Demand for interceptors has climbed sharply in recent years. U.S. warships have expended SM-series missiles in real combat defending against missile and drone attacks in the Middle East, and replenishing those magazines has become a stated priority for Navy leadership.

Neither the Pentagon nor Raytheon has disclosed the exact number of missiles covered or the delivery schedule. As with most large munitions contracts, the ceiling value represents the maximum the government can order over the life of the agreement.
