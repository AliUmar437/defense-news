Pentagon, Lockheed Weigh Multi-Year F-35 Production and Sustainment Deal

By Robert HayesOct 09, 2026 10:18 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

The Pentagon and Lockheed Martin are negotiating a multi-year agreement covering F-35 production and sustainment, a deal that could reshape the economics of the world's largest fighter program.

The talks, first reported by Breaking Defense, would bundle several years of aircraft buys and long-term support into a single contract — the kind of arrangement the Pentagon favors for driving down unit costs through stable, predictable orders.

But skepticism is already surfacing. One source familiar with the negotiations expressed doubt that Lockheed "can successfully propose something that demonstrates long-term cost savings," a pointed reminder that the F-35's sustainment bill — the cost of keeping the fleet flying — has been the program's most stubborn problem for a decade.

With more than 1,000 F-35s delivered worldwide and production still running, even small percentage shifts in sustainment costs translate into tens of billions of dollars over the fleet's lifetime. That is what makes these negotiations some of the highest-stakes contracting in the defense world.

No timeline for an agreement has been announced.
