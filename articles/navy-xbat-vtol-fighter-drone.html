Navy Doubles Down on X-BAT VTOL 'Fighter' Drone

James CarterOct 08, 2026 18:26 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

The Navy is doubling down on the X-BAT, a vertical-takeoff-and-landing drone being developed to give fighter-like capability to ships that lack catapults and arresting gear.

Flight testing is approaching, and the program has laid out what The War Zone describes as an extremely ambitious timeline to reach operational capability. Shield AI, the autonomy company behind the design, has drawn major investment — reportedly reaching $400 million with U.S. government backing.

X-BAT's trick is its tail-sitter design: it takes off and lands vertically like a helicopter, then transitions to winged forward flight. That means it could operate from amphibious ships, destroyers, or expeditionary airfields — places no conventional fighter drone can go.

The Navy wants unmanned aircraft that can scout, strike, and jam from distributed ships across the Pacific. A VTOL fighter drone fits that vision precisely.

Ambitious timelines in drone development often slip. But the money flowing into X-BAT shows how seriously the Navy takes the concept.
