Northrop Grumman's Talon Blue Drone Takes Flight in CCA Race

James CarterOct 03, 2026 18:31 UTC 3 min readRegion: United States

Northrop Grumman's Talon Blue — now carrying the official designation YFQ-48A — has made its first flight, entering the Air Force's collaborative combat aircraft race as flying hardware rather than a viewgraph.

The company announced Oct. 3 that the uncrewed aircraft completed its first fully autonomous flight over the Mojave Air and Space Port in California, including taxi, takeoff, in-flight maneuvers, and landing. The aircraft was built by Scaled Composites, Northrop Grumman's subsidiary known for experimental designs, which developed it largely in secrecy before a formal unveiling at Mojave in December 2025. The Air Force assigned the YFQ-48A designation around the turn of the year — in the Pentagon's designation system, Y marks a prototype, F a fighter mission, and Q an unmanned aircraft.

Northrop developed Talon Blue entirely with company funds under its internally-funded Project Talon portfolio. The pitch centers on cost and production speed: the company says manufacturing and design innovations cut part count and overall weight, lowering cost and enabling faster production. The aircraft's high engine intake, wide-set landing gear, and integrated internal weapons bay are designed to support a range of missions for U.S. and allied customers. It is powered by a Pratt & Whitney PW500-series turbofan — an engine family better known from business jets — and flew under the civil registration N444LX, reaching about 233 knots and just over 10,000 feet on its first flight, according to flight-tracking data cited by Aviation Week.

Craig Woolston, Northrop's vice president and general manager for research and advanced design, said customers had made clear they need autonomous systems that can be fielded faster and more affordably without sacrificing mission effectiveness. "We listened," he said. "Talon Blue's first flight is one step toward a new generation of autonomous capabilities."

Chasing Increment 2

Talon Blue enters a crowded field. The Air Force has moved its Increment 1 CCAs — Anduril's YFQ-44 and General Atomics' YFQ-42 — into production after deep flight testing, and the service plans to field at least 500 CCAs by 2032. Talon Blue is aimed at the follow-on Increment 2 acquisition, whose requirements have not yet been defined.

The competition includes Boeing's MQ-28 Ghost Bat, Lockheed Martin's tailless Vectis, and Kratos' FQ-58 Valkyrie, alongside offerings from new players and foreign manufacturers. For Northrop, a win matters beyond the drone program itself: with the F/A-XX next-generation naval fighter award going to Boeing, a CCA contract would bring the company back into the Pentagon's tactical air combat portfolio.

Northrop has also emphasized the autonomy ecosystem behind the aircraft, including its Model 437-based Talon IQ testbed — formerly known as Beacon — which matures autonomous mission software more cheaply than full-scale prototypes. The company cites seven decades of autonomy experience and more than 500,000 autonomous flight hours feeding into the program.

The broader bet is that advanced uncrewed tactical aircraft will be less risky and potentially more lucrative than traditional fighter programs, as air combat shifts toward predominantly uncrewed capabilities built in large numbers. Talon Blue's first autonomous flight is an early step — but in a race the Air Force is running at speed, flying hardware counts.
