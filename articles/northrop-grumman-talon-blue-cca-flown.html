Northrop Grumman's Talon Blue Drone Takes Flight in CCA Race

By Defense News StaffOct 03, 2026 18:31 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

Northrop Grumman's Talon Blue has taken flight, as the defense giant angles for a share of the Air Force's Collaborative Combat Aircraft portfolio.

The first flight, reported by The War Zone, puts Northrop's design in the air at a moment when the CCA program is accelerating. The Air Force wants potentially thousands of AI-piloted drones to fly alongside crewed fighters, and multiple vendors are competing to define what those aircraft look like.

Talon Blue's exact configuration remains closely held, but the CCA mission set is public: loyal-wingman drones that carry extra weapons, extend sensor coverage, jam enemy defenses, and take on the riskiest missions ahead of manned fighters.

Northrop, which builds the B-21 Raider stealth bomber, is betting its experience with advanced, survivable aircraft translates to the drone fight. It faces competition from General Atomics, Anduril, and others already flying CCA demonstrators.

With the Air Force now naming its first CCAs — the FQ-42 Vengeance and FQ-44 Fury — the race is shifting from viewgraphs to flying hardware.
