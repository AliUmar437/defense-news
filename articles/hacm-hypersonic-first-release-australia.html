USAF's New HACM Hypersonic Cruise Missile Makes First Release Over Australian Range

By Defense News StaffOct 05, 2026 23:27 UTC 1 min readRegion: Indo-Pacific

The U.S. Air Force has conducted the first release of its new Hypersonic Attack Cruise Missile (HACM) over a test range in Australia, putting the long-delayed weapon into flight testing at last.

The test, reported by The War Zone, is a joint U.S.-Australian effort. Australia's vast, sparsely populated test ranges have become the proving ground of choice for Western hypersonic programs, offering the long flight corridors the weapons need.

HACM is an air-breathing hypersonic cruise missile, designed to fly at more than five times the speed of sound while maneuvering in the atmosphere — a combination that makes it extremely hard to intercept. It is meant to give bombers and fighters a conventional strike weapon that can penetrate defended airspace.

The program has suffered major delays, and the successful release marks a turning point. The Pentagon is racing to field operational hypersonic weapons as China and Russia deploy their own, and flight-test data from HACM will feed directly into follow-on designs.

Neither the Air Force nor its Australian partners has released details of the test outcome.
