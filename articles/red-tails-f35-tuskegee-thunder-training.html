'Red Tails' Train to Keep F-35s Flying Under Fire

Robert HayesAug 17, 2026 16:56 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

Airmen of the 187th Fighter Wing — the Alabama Air National Guard unit carrying the legendary "Red Tails" name — have been training to generate and sustain F-35A combat airpower under pressure during Operation Tuskegee Thunder at Dannelly Field.

The exercise drilled the unglamorous side of airpower: turning jets quickly, fixing them fast, and operating from austere locations while under simulated attack, according to the Defense Department. Modern air wars are won as much by maintainers and logisticians as by pilots.

The Red Tails name honors the Tuskegee Airmen, the Black fighter pilots of World War II whose escort record became legend. Today's 187th flies the F-35A Lightning II, the Air Force's stealth workhorse.

The focus on dispersed, resilient operations reflects hard lessons from Ukraine and the Pacific: concentrated airbases are targets, and the side that keeps flying after the first strike wins.

The wing regularly deploys worldwide in support of combatant commanders.
