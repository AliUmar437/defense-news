One Navy Squadron's Staggering Weapons Bill from the War with Iran

David SullivanOct 02, 2026 22:49 UTC 3 min readRegion: United States

A single Navy fighter squadron fired 421 missiles and bombs during Operation Epic Fury — and someone kept count.

A photograph of VFA-41's expenditure board, shared by aviation photographer Jeff Rojas, lays out the "Black Aces'" full wartime bill from the U.S. air campaign against Iran: 173 GBU-38 500-pound JDAMs, 52 GBU-31(V)4 bunker-buster 2,000-pounders, 48 dual-mode laser JDAMs, 41 JSOW glide bombs, 33 AIM-9X Sidewinders, 30 standard 2,000-pound JDAMs, 27 1,000-pound JDAMs, 10 SLAM-ER cruise missiles, 6 AIM-120 AMRAAMs, and a single GBU-12 laser-guided bomb.

The War Zone, which first published the tally, estimates the ordnance cost at roughly $43.6 million at minimum — derived from U.S. military budget documents, with the caveat that munitions pricing fluctuates and JDAM kit costs are hard to separate from the warheads they guide.

Two months of war

VFA-41 flies the F/A-18F Super Hornet as part of the air wing aboard USS Abraham Lincoln. The Pentagon says Operation Epic Fury ran from Feb. 28 to May 5, 2026 — about nine weeks — though American strikes on Iranian targets continued intermittently afterward. Lincoln, on a marathon deployment stretching back to 2025, had just left the Middle East when the tally surfaced in early October, bound for home in San Diego.

The air-to-air portion of the list is its own story. Thirty-three AIM-9Xs and six AMRAAMs with no known kills of crewed Iranian aircraft — the missiles were almost certainly fired at Iranian drones, and possibly cruise missiles. After a crash program in 2024, Super Hornets can now carry up to nine air-to-air missiles per sortie, a configuration crews call the "Murder Hornet" loadout, built specifically for the drone threat.

The expenditure has reignited Washington's munitions debate. Precision weapons take years to build and cannot be surged overnight; Pentagon planners have warned for years that a high-end fight would burn through stockpiles faster than industry can replace them. Epic Fury — nine weeks, one squadron, 421 weapons — is now the freshest data point in that argument. Multiply the Black Aces' bill across Lincoln's four strike squadrons, the rest of the joint force, and the months of intermittent strikes that followed, and the industrial question answers itself.

Markings of a drone war

The squadron's jets came home decorated. One VFA-41 Super Hornet, Bureau Number 168491, returned to Naval Air Station Lemoore on Oct. 1 with a broken-submarine silhouette painted under the cockpit labeled "Sub Buster" — believed to mark a kill on an Iranian submarine, a first for the type. Iranian Kilo-class and Ghadir midget submarines were priority targets throughout the campaign, and the Pentagon has previously confirmed submarine kills by other means, including an MQ-9's Hellfire and an Army ATACMS strike.

The Black Aces were not alone. A VMFA-314 F-35C "Black Knights" jet returned with its own battle markings: an oil tanker, three submarines, a surface combatant, a Shahed-type drone, artillery, and launcher silhouettes, with tally marks beside AIM-120, AGM-154, GBU-31, GBU-54, and GBU-32 silhouettes.

For scale: the entire Eisenhower strike group fired 770 munitions across eight months of Red Sea fighting in 2023-2024. One squadron fired 421 in about two. The Pentagon has released relatively little official data on Epic Fury's ordnance expenditure — which is precisely why a squadron scoreboard photographed by a civilian spotter became news. It is worth stating plainly: the 421 figure comes from that photograph, not from a Navy release.
