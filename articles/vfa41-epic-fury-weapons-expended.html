One Navy Squadron's Staggering Weapons Bill from the War with Iran

David SullivanOct 02, 2026 22:49 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

Strike Fighter Squadron 41, the "Black Aces," expended hundreds of air-to-air and air-to-surface weapons during Operation Epic Fury — the war with Iran — according to a remarkable accounting reported by The War Zone.

The tally from a single F/A-18F Super Hornet squadron illustrates the sheer scale of the air campaign: hundreds of precision bombs, air-to-air missiles, and other munitions in weeks of combat. Multiply that across the carrier air wing, and the expenditure becomes staggering.

The numbers also highlight a strategic worry: munitions stockpiles. Precision weapons take years to build, and a short, intense war can burn through reserves faster than industry replaces them. Pentagon planners have been warning about exactly this.

VFA-41's Super Hornets flew strike and fighter missions from the carrier deck around the clock, a tempo that tested both the jets and their crews.

The full accounting offers a rare window into what modern naval air warfare actually costs in ordnance.
