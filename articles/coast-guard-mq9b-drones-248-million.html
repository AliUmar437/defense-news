Coast Guard Orders Its First MQ-9B Drones in $248 Million Deal

David SullivanOct 09, 2026 20:10 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

The U.S. Coast Guard has placed a $248 million order for its first MQ-9B drones, bringing long-endurance unmanned surveillance to the maritime service.

The unarmed aircraft are meant to "give the Coast Guard crews a longer-range view of our maritime domain," a senior official said, according to Breaking Defense. The MQ-9B — the same family as the Air Force's Reaper — can stay airborne for more than 30 hours, scanning vast ocean areas for drug smugglers, illegal fishing, and distressed vessels.

For the Coast Guard, which patrols enormous areas with a small fleet of ships and aircraft, persistence is everything. A single drone can watch a smuggling corridor for a day and a half without refueling, cueing cutters to intercept.

The buy also reflects the MQ-9B's evolution into a multi-mission platform. Beyond the Coast Guard, variants serve with the Royal Air Force, and the Navy is eyeing carrier-based derivatives.

Deliveries and basing plans have not been announced.
