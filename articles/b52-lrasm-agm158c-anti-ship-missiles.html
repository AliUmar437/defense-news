B-52 to Get Stealthy LRASM Anti-Ship Missiles, Completing the Bomber Trio

David SullivanOct 09, 2026 17:59 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

The B-52 Stratofortress is getting the AGM-158C Long Range Anti-Ship Missile, completing the integration of the stealthy weapon across all three of America's heavy bombers.

With LRASM on the B-52, the B-1B, and the B-2 already able to carry it — and the future B-21 certain to follow — the Air Force can now hold enemy warships at risk from any of its bomber platforms, The War Zone reports.

LRASM is a subsonic, stealthy cruise missile designed to hunt ships autonomously in contested waters. Launched from hundreds of miles away, it uses onboard sensors to find and identify targets even when GPS and datalinks are jammed — exactly the conditions expected in a Pacific fight.

For the B-52, the pairing is potent. The 70-year-old bomber can carry a large load of cruise missiles and launch them from well outside enemy air defenses, turning it into what strategists call an "arsenal plane" for maritime strike.

The move reflects the Pentagon's focus on China, whose navy is now the world's largest by hull count. Sinking or deterring that fleet from long range is a central U.S. war plan.
