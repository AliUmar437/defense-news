B-1B Bombers Beat a Hasty Retreat from England Amid Iran Threats

Michael ReynoldsOct 05, 2026 01:04 UTC 3 min readRegion: Europe

U.S. Air Force B-1B Lancer bombers have made a hasty departure from RAF Fairford in England, pulling out of the forward base that hosted them during this year's combat operations against Iran after commanders received new intelligence about an Iran-backed threat to the installation.

All 12 of the B-1s stationed at the Gloucestershire airfield left over the weekend, local aircraft spotters and aviation photographers reported, with the jets departing in several waves on Sunday, Oct. 4. A U.S. defense official confirmed the withdrawal to The War Zone, and the Pentagon later acknowledged that every American bomber deployed to Fairford had re-deployed to home stations in the United States. It was the first time in months that the base hosted no U.S. bombers.

A threat that escalated in hours

The withdrawal was highly unusual for an allied base in a peacetime country thousands of miles from a combat zone. The Times reported that some bombers and their crews departed while aerial refueling tankers were still being scrambled to support the transatlantic flight — a measure of how quickly the order came. At least three of the Lancers had already landed at their home base in South Dakota by Sunday.

According to the Times, commanders acted after receiving new threats about an Iran-backed plot against the installation — warnings that had been building for three weeks before urgency spiked in the previous 24 hours. The Pentagon would not confirm the nature of the intelligence, and the Wall Street Journal was the first to report the departure.

President Donald Trump confirmed on Monday that the threats drove the decision. "They didn't fly out by accident," he told reporters outside the White House. "There were some threats." He added that the United States knew the people behind them. Vice President JD Vance also confirmed the move and accused Iran of plotting attacks against the United States, while Secretary of State Marco Rubio pointed to the "hands of a foreign actor."

A week of arrests near the base

The evacuation followed a string of arrests around Fairford. On Sept. 27, five men were detained near the base on suspicion of driving vans toward it. Gasoline was found in the vehicles but no explosives, and all five were released on bail. On Oct. 1, a 25-year-old British-Iranian dual national was arrested in London on suspicion of preparing terrorist acts and released on bail; days later, a 22-year-old was arrested in Westminster. No one has been charged.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said there were strong indications that Iran was behind the suspected plot. Iran denied the accusations, called them baseless, and summoned the British ambassador in Tehran over the matter.

The base was already under a spotlight. Iran had openly threatened Fairford in July, after the airfield's role in strikes on Iranian targets became clear. "Any base that is used to invade Iran's territory is our legitimate goal," Iran's Tasnim News Agency claimed on Telegram on July 23, referring to B-1s flying from Fairford.

Fairford's role in the Iran campaign

Fairford has been a central hub of the U.S. bomber campaign against Iran. The base hosted a mix of B-1s and B-52s earlier this year, with the total bomber presence at the Gloucestershire hub peaking at 21 to 23 strategic bombers. Six B-52s returned across the Atlantic in a drawdown late last month, leaving about a dozen B-1s — some of which had arrived only days earlier as part of a normal force rotation — to be evacuated.

Stationing bombers at Fairford sharply reduced the flight time needed to reach targets in Iran. The Pentagon emphasized that the withdrawal does not affect readiness.

"The Airmen that operate America's bomber force, including B-1s, B-2s, and B-52s, remain ready to deliver precision global strike capabilities — including through operations that launch and recover from the continental United States — anywhere, anytime," the Pentagon said. Both B-1s, B-2s, and B-52s have demonstrated those long-range strikes during this year's operations.

The bombers' repositioning may only be temporary. Fairford remains the U.S. military's forward bomber hub in the United Kingdom — and a fresh reminder that the facilities behind the Iran campaign are themselves in Tehran's sights.
