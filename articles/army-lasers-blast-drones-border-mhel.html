Army Lasers Blast Cartel Drones Out of the Sky in Border Test

Michael ReynoldsAug 28, 2026 22:02 UTC 1 min readRegion: United States

An Army high-energy laser has shot down three hostile drones near the southern border, in one of the clearest real-world demonstrations yet of directed-energy weapons.

Service members assigned to Joint Task Force Southern Border used the Army's Multipurpose High Energy Laser to stop three unmanned aircraft deemed a threat, according to the Defense Department. The drones were believed linked to drug cartels using them for smuggling surveillance.

The engagement is a milestone for laser weapons, which have been "five years away" for decades. Unlike missile interceptors costing hundreds of thousands of dollars per shot, a laser kills for roughly the price of the diesel fuel running its generator — as long as it has power and a clear line of sight.

Cartel drones have become a genuine security problem, spotting for smugglers and probing border defenses. A weapon that can drop them cheaply and repeatedly changes the economics of that fight.

The Army did not say whether the laser will deploy permanently to the border.
